Dry van dispatch service

Dry vans are the most common trailer on the road, which means plenty of freight and plenty of competition. A good dispatcher helps you stay loaded, avoid deadhead miles and get paid fairly for your time.

What dry vans haul

Packaged and palletized freight such as consumer goods, retail and grocery shelf-stable products, paper and general merchandise, usually in a 53-foot trailer with roughly 42,000 to 45,000 lbs of capacity.

Where a dispatcher makes you money

Because dry van freight is competitive, small differences add up: negotiating rates above the first offer, planning backhauls before you deliver, choosing lanes where you can reload quickly, and avoiding markets where trucks get stuck.

Detention and layovers

Check the free time written into each rate confirmation and make sure detention pay is documented. We track arrival times and chase unpaid detention so waiting at a dock doesn't come out of your pocket.

Drop-and-hook and live loads

Drop-and-hook loads keep your truck moving, while live loads can eat hours. We tell you which one you're booking before you accept.

Frequently asked questions

How much freight can a dry van carry?

A standard 53-foot dry van typically carries around 42,000 to 45,000 lbs, depending on the trailer and tractor setup.

Do you plan backhauls for dry van loads?

Yes. We look at the return lane before you accept the outbound load.

Do I approve every dry van load?

Yes. You approve every load before we book it.
