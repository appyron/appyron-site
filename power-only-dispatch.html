Power-only dispatch service

Power-only means you haul a trailer you don't own, so your tractor spends less time waiting. It can keep you moving, but only if the load, trailer and paperwork are right.

How power-only works

You hook to a trailer provided by a broker, shipper or carrier, deliver it and drop it. Many loads are drop-and-hook, which cuts loading time.

Paperwork and insurance

Hauling someone else's trailer usually requires a trailer interchange agreement and the right coverage for non-owned trailers. We check what's required before you accept a load.

Check the trailer first

Inspect tires, brakes, lights and doors at pickup and document any damage, so problems don't get blamed on you later.

Why dispatch matters

Rates are often structured differently from standard loads. We compare them per mile and per day so you can see which loads actually keep your tractor profitable.

Frequently asked questions

What is power-only trucking?

Power-only means a tractor hauls a trailer owned by someone else, often in drop-and-hook loads.

Do I need special insurance for power-only?

Usually you need coverage that applies to non-owned trailers. We confirm requirements before you accept a load.

Do power-only loads pay less?

Rates can differ, but quick turns can make up for it. We compare loads per mile and per day.
